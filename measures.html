Stage 1 · the three layers

Where does protection sit?

Organisations defend data in three overlapping layers. Physical controls protect the building and the people in it. Hardware controls protect the devices and the data on them. Software controls protect the systems and the network. Most real security needs all three, because an attacker only has to find the gap.

Sort each measure into the layer it mostly belongs to. Some genuinely straddle two — pick the best fit, and read why afterwards.

Stage 2 · right measure, wrong threat

Six incidents. What actually helps?

For each incident, decide whether each measure would genuinely help against that specific threat, or whether it's the wrong tool — however good it is at its own job. Mark every option, then reveal the verdict. Watch for the expensive controls that do nothing here.

Stage 3 · no single control is enough

One company, three threats at once

A 30-person company is worried about three things this year: staff falling for phishing, a possible ransomware infection, and an out-of-hours break-in to steal equipment. No single measure covers all three. Choose a set that covers each threat at least once — and notice how a good answer spans all three layers.

Evaluation · cost versus effectiveness

So what's worth the money?

You've now seen that the most expensive measure is worthless against the wrong threat, that no single control is enough, and that the right mix changes with the organisation. Use that to make a judgement.

Exam-style evaluation

An organisation has a limited budget and wants to protect sensitive customer data. Evaluate the extent to which it should prioritise physical security measures over hardware and software measures. Refer to specific threats, compare the measures, and reach a justified recommendation.

A strong answer will:

  • show that each measure only counters certain threats, with examples from today's incidents;
  • weigh cost against effectiveness — an expensive control that misses the real threat is poor value;
  • argue for a layered mix across physical, hardware and software rather than one category;
  • tie the recommendation to the organisation's actual data, threats and budget.
